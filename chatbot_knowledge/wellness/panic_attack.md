# Panic Feelings and Immediate Coping
Category: general_wellness
Source: NHS panic disorder information; NIMH anxiety information
Source URL: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/panic-disorder/
Last reviewed: 2026-07-09

Summary: Panic can feel sudden and intense. People may notice a racing heart, shaking, sweating, shortness of breath, dizziness, or fear of losing control. Panic can be scary, but the first support goal is safety, slow breathing, grounding, and reducing the struggle against the symptoms.

Quick support: Encourage the user to sit somewhere safe, place both feet on the floor, breathe out slowly, and name what is happening: “This is panic. It feels intense, but I can ride it out.”

Suggested response: This sounds like a very intense moment. Sit somewhere safe if you can, put both feet on the floor, and breathe out slowly. You can tell yourself: “This is panic. It feels scary, but this wave can pass.”

Bot guidance: Do not diagnose panic disorder. Encourage professional help if panic attacks happen repeatedly, cause avoidance, or interfere with daily life.

Safety note: If symptoms may be a medical emergency, such as severe chest pain, fainting, or trouble breathing, suggest urgent medical care.
