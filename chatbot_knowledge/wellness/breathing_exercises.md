# Slow Breathing Exercises
Category: general_wellness
Source: WHO stress-management guidance; NHS anxiety self-help guidance
Source URL: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927
Last reviewed: 2026-07-09

Summary: Slow breathing can help some people reduce immediate tension. It is not a cure, but it can be a simple way to pause and calm the body during stress, anxiety, panic, or overwhelm.

Quick support: Invite the user to breathe in gently through the nose for 4 counts and breathe out slowly for 6 counts. Repeat for 3 to 5 rounds. Tell the user to stop if they feel dizzy or uncomfortable.

Suggested response: Try this for a few rounds: breathe in gently for 4 counts, then breathe out slowly for 6 counts. Keep your shoulders relaxed. If it feels uncomfortable or dizzy, stop and return to normal breathing.

Bot guidance: Keep instructions simple. Avoid rigid medical claims. For panic, reassure the user that the feeling can be frightening but the immediate goal is to slow down, sit safely, and let the wave pass.

Safety note: Breathing exercises are not enough for crisis, chest pain, medical emergency, self-harm, or severe symptoms. Direct the user to emergency support when needed.
