# Exam and Study Stress
Category: general_wellness
Source: WHO stress-management principles; NHS anxiety guidance
Source URL: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927
Last reviewed: 2026-07-09

Summary: Exam stress can create worry, avoidance, sleep difficulty, irritability, and racing thoughts. Support should focus on breaking tasks down, short study blocks, realistic planning, rest, and help-seeking.

Quick support: Suggest a 25-minute study block, one topic only, phone away, followed by a 5-minute break. Encourage the user to focus on the next action rather than the whole exam.

Suggested response: Exam stress can make the whole future feel like it depends on one moment. For now, choose one topic and study it for 25 minutes with your phone away, then take a 5-minute break. The goal is progress, not perfection.

Bot guidance: Encourage routines, sleep, food, and support from teachers/friends/family. Do not guarantee results. If anxiety is severe or causing panic, suggest professional support.

Safety note: If exam stress includes self-harm or suicidal thoughts, use the crisis response.
