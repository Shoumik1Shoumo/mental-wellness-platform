# Anxiety and Grounding
Category: general_wellness
Source: NHS anxiety/fear/panic guidance; WHO stress-management guidance
Source URL: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/feelings-and-symptoms/anxiety-fear-panic/
Last reviewed: 2026-07-09

Summary: Anxiety can feel like worry, fear, physical tension, fast breathing, racing thoughts, or a sense that something bad may happen. Occasional anxiety is common, but support is important when anxiety is frequent, intense, or affects daily life.

Quick support: Help the user slow down and reconnect with the present moment. Suggest naming five things they can see, four things they can feel, three things they can hear, two things they can smell, and one thing they can taste. Encourage a slow exhale and a small next step.

Suggested response: That sounds really uncomfortable. Try grounding yourself for one minute: name 5 things you can see, 4 things you can feel, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, and 1 thing you can taste. Then take a slow breath out and choose one small safe thing to do next.

Bot guidance: Validate first. Do not diagnose. Do not say the user has an anxiety disorder. Suggest professional support when anxiety is persistent, severe, or interfering with school, work, sleep, relationships, or daily life.

Safety note: If the user mentions self-harm, suicide, violence, or inability to stay safe, ignore this document and use the crisis response.
